15. Iran in the Mirror of the Global Labour Market (2022–2024)

4 min read · 2 tables · 1 figures · 1 code cells

Global reports for this period have three clear patterns: a hiring surge after Corona and then layoffs in technology in 2023, the consolidation of hybrid remote work, and rapid growth in demand for data and artificial intelligence skills. In this section, we measure these same patterns with proxy indicators in the JobVision data. The comparison is qualitative and its aim is to find similarities and divergences, not to prove causality.

Interpretation of tables 15.1 and 15.2 and figure 15.1: The proxy indicators show a picture different from the global narrative. The share of the information technology industry fell from 9.2% to 7.7% and the share of the programming category from 5.2% to 3.5%, while in the same period the global market saw growing demand for technology and artificial intelligence. The remote work rate remained almost constant (6.7% to 7%).

The share of data and artificial intelligence skills in this data is very small: fewer than 1% of the postings mention skills such as Python or Keras, and terms such as TensorFlow, PyTorch or “machine learning” are absent from the data skill list. This limitation is important: JobVision's skill list is not complete with respect to emerging technologies, so our figure is a floor for artificial intelligence skill demand, not its actual size.

On the wage side, technology tracks sit above the market median: programming, with a median of 17.5 million, is about +59% above the overall median; network and DevOps and digital marketing are at or slightly above the market median.

15.2. Comparative validity against global trends #

Table 15.3 | Global trends 2022–2024 against the evidence of the Iranian market

Global trend Evidence in the JobVision data Reading
Post-COVID hiring surge and then tech layoffs in 2023 Growth in the number of postings up to Mehr 1402 and the change in the information-technology industry's share in Table 15.1 Iran experienced neither a great surge nor a sharp tech correction; demand has been gradual and continuous.
Consolidation of hybrid remote work as the norm The remote-work rate in Table 15.1 and the provincial dispersion in Section 14 In Iran remote work is a marginal commodity, not the norm; instead, multi-province postings and branches have replaced working from a distance.
Exponential growth in demand for AI and data skills The share of data and AI skills in Table 15.1 The direction of growth is in line with the world, but the absolute level is far lower; a digital skills divide is evident.
Shortage of skilled labour and the technology salary premium The ratio of information-technology salaries to other industries in Table 15.2 The technology salary premium in Iran is persistent; the market rewards scarce skill.
Geographic concentration of talent and competition among cities Tehran's share and the provincial change in Section 14 Tehran's concentration is far more extreme than the global pattern; Iran's labour market is monocentric.
Salary transparency as an employer's competitive tool The 33% disclosure rate and the findings of Section 9 A large gap with transparent markets; the opportunity for differentiation for an Iranian employer is serious.

Limitation: The figures in global reports (Indeed Hiring Lab and LinkedIn Economic Graph) come from external sources and are for qualitative comparison; this notebook does not reproduce them. The indicators in Table 15.1 are domestic substitutes, not exact equivalents of those reports.

Comparison summary: The directions align, but the level and pace differ. In the 1401–1402 period Iran experienced neither a great technology surge nor a sharp correction; instead, the market pivoted towards executive, sales and digital marketing roles. Remote work did not become the norm, and salary transparency also stayed at the 33% level. Three key divergences from the world: the low and declining share of technology, the absence of an AI-skills wave, and the extreme geographic concentration in Tehran.

For an Iranian manager, this means that “copying the global version of talent acquisition” does not work; the focus should be on salary transparency, tangible benefits, and recruiting non-Tehrani talent with a remote-work model.
