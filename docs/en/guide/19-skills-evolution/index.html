Interpretation of Table 19.1 and Figure 19.1: Over two years, the market's skill basket has changed slowly but directionally. The largest gains: WordPress +1.4 percentage points, Microsoft Word +1.1, Google Analytics +1.0, Sepidar +0.8 and Excel +0.7. The largest declines: Microsoft Outlook −4.2, PowerPoint −4.0, Microsoft Dynamics −1.1, Microsoft Project −1.0, HTML/CSS −0.8 and JavaScript −0.7.

Two narratives emerge from these numbers. First, a shift toward digital marketing: WordPress, Google Analytics and Keyword Planner have grown, which is consistent with the growing share of digital marketing in Section 10. Second, the replacement of heavy office tools: Outlook, PowerPoint and Project have given way to lighter, communication-oriented tools; part of this decline may stem from a change in how skills are recorded in postings rather than a real drop in demand.

Warning: programming skills (JavaScript, HTML/CSS, C#) have declined in share, in parallel with the decline in the share of the programming category. These two data points confirm each other.

Interpretation of Table 19.2 and Figure 19.2: Requiring at least 50 postings in each of the two years (to remove explosive small-sample growth), the fastest growth belongs to barista (+422%), cook (+318%), Instagram admin (+309%), packaging worker (+295%), website designer (+264%) and safety officer (+259%). On the decline side: insurance representative (−77%), regional sales manager (−67%), marketing and advertising officer (−43%) and sales trainee (−40%).

This pattern is consistent with structural changes in Iran's market: growth in consumer services (café, restaurant, packaging) and social-media jobs, and a decline in insurance sales roles and regional sales management. An important point: “Instagram admin” and “social media admin” are two separate titles that were not merged during normalization; therefore the real growth of this area is larger than the figure for any single title.

19.3. Has the skill basket of a fixed title changed? #

title_skill_share = (skills_long.merge(ads[['ad_id', 'base_title', 'jyear']], on='ad_id')
                     .groupby(['base_title', 'jyear', 'skill']).ad_id.nunique().unstack(fill_value=0))
title_ads_year = (ads.assign(has_skill=ads.ad_id.isin(skills_long.ad_id))
                  .groupby(['base_title', 'jyear']).has_skill.sum())
title_skill_rate = title_skill_share.div(title_ads_year, axis=0) * 100

stability_rows = []
top_titles_by_volume = ads.base_title.value_counts().head(12).index
for title in top_titles_by_volume:
    try:
        first = set(title_skill_rate.loc[(title, 1401)].dropna().loc[lambda s: s.ge(10)].index)
        second = set(title_skill_rate.loc[(title, 1402)].dropna().loc[lambda s: s.ge(10)].index)
    except KeyError:
        continue
    if not first or not second:
        continue
    jaccard = len(first & second) / len(first | second)
    joined = pd.DataFrame({'۱۴۰۱': title_skill_rate.loc[(title, 1401)],
                           '۱۴۰۲': title_skill_rate.loc[(title, 1402)]}).dropna()
    joined['تغییر'] = joined['۱۴۰۲'] - joined['۱۴۰۱']
    stability_rows.append({
        'عنوان شغلی': title, 'تعداد آگهی': int(ads.base_title.eq(title).sum()),
        'شباهت سبد مهارت': jaccard,
        'مهارت در حال رشد': joined['تغییر'].idxmax() if len(joined) else np.nan,
        'رشد مهارت (واحد درصدی)': joined['تغییر'].max() if len(joined) else np.nan,
        'مهارت در حال افت': joined['تغییر'].idxmin() if len(joined) else np.nan,
        'افت مهارت (واحد درصدی)': joined['تغییر'].min() if len(joined) else np.nan,
    })
title_stability = pd.DataFrame(stability_rows).set_index('عنوان شغلی').sort_values('تعداد آگهی', ascending=False)
show_table('19.3', 'پایداری سبد مهارت در پرتکرارترین عناوین شغلی', title_stability,
           formats={'تعداد آگهی': 'int', 'شباهت سبد مهارت': 'ratio', 'رشد مهارت (واحد درصدی)': 'pp',
                    'افت مهارت (واحد درصدی)': 'pp'},
           sample='مهارت‌های با سهم دست‌کم ۱۰٪ در هر سال', period=False, index_name='عنوان شغلی',
           note='شباهت جاکارد ۱ یعنی سبد مهارت‌های پرتقاضا کاملاً ثابت مانده و ۰ یعنی کاملاً عوض شده است.')

show_metrics('تحول مهارت و عنوان', [
    ('میانه شباهت سبد مهارت', f'{title_stability["شباهت سبد مهارت"].median():.2f}'),
    ('بیشترین رشد مهارت', f'{risers.index[0]} ({risers["تغییر واحد درصدی"].iloc[0]:+.2f} واحد)'),
    ('بیشترین افت مهارت', f'{fallers.index[0]} ({fallers["تغییر واحد درصدی"].iloc[0]:+.2f} واحد)'),
    ('سریع‌ترین رشد عنوان', f'{rising_titles.index[0]} ({rising_titles["رشد تعداد"].iloc[0]:+,.0f}٪)'),
])

Key figures: skill and title evolution

  • Median skill-set similarity: 0.92
  • Largest skill growth: Wordpress (+1.38 points)
  • Largest skill decline: Microsoft Outlook (-4.17 points)
  • Fastest-growing title: barista (+422%)

Interpretation of Table 19.3: The skill basket of the most frequent titles is stable: the median Jaccard similarity is 0.92. Accountant (0.83), telephone sales officer (0.60) and warehouse keeper (0.71) have changed the most, but the main change is inside the very same existing skills: Excel up, Outlook down, and the gradual arrival of local software (Sepidar, Rahkaran, Hamkaran System).

In other words, a “job title” is a stable container whose skill content changes slowly; that is why the clustering of Section 17 remained stable over the two years.
