2. Data, data culture and analysis rules

6 min read · 1015 words

2.1. Source and specifications #

The data is obtained from JobVision's public Hugging Face repository. Each row is one published job posting; not a company, not a job seeker and not a successful hire. This distinction is critical for interpreting all the results.

Table 2.1 | Specifications of the raw data

Characteristic Value Description
Source JobVision Jobposts Dataset public release on Hugging Face
Number of rows 364,838 each row is one posting
Number of columns 43 text, number, boolean and JSON
Time range March 2022 to March 2024 monthly granularity
Complete months 23 months the first and last months are incomplete
Salary unit million tomans per month inferred from the range of values; examined in section 5
Salary disclosure rate about 33% only this subset undergoes salary analysis
License CC BY-NC 4.0 research and non-commercial, with source attribution

2.4. What is not in the data #

Attention to the “absences” matters as much as attention to the “presences”:

  • The full posting text and job description are not present; only the title, skills, benefits and hiring requirements.
  • Company name has been anonymized; competition analysis is done at the level of company attributes, not brand.
  • The exact publication date is not present; only the month.
  • Job seeker information, successful hires, actual contract salaries and retention rates are not present.
  • Inflation adjustment is not in the data; values are nominal and time comparisons must be interpreted with an inflation index.
  • Hourly wage is not recorded directly; it must be derived from monthly salary and working hours.

2.5. Salary unit: why million tomans? #

The data card states the salary unit as “Toman”, but the range of values (2 to 100 with a median of 10) is not consistent with raw “toman”: a monthly salary of 10 tomans is meaningless. Three pieces of evidence show that the real scale is million tomans per month:

  1. The median of the declared minimum salary is 10 and the median maximum is 12; this is consistent with the market wage level in 1401–1402.
  2. The 99th percentile of the minimum salary is 35 and the maximum is 70; a range that is reasonable for managers and specialized roles in those years.
  3. If the scale were “thousand tomans”, a median of 10 thousand tomans per month would not be consistent with any standard.

This assumption is applied uniformly throughout the notebook. If the data scale changes, all salary numbers must be recomputed.

2.6. Pre-specified analysis rules #

To prevent discretionary decisions taken after seeing the result, the following rules are set before the analysis:

Table 2.4 | Fixed analysis rules

Topic Rule Reason
Main period 2022-04 to 2024-02 removing the two incomplete months at the start and end
Salary unit million tomans per month consistency with external evidence
Significance level 0.05 hypothesis tests
Minimum sample for a table 30 stability of percentages and medians
Threshold for frequent categories 100 postings sector comparison
Outliers kept in the description, winsorized 1–99% only in the model avoiding the loss of information
Missing salary analyzed separately, without dropping rows the missingness itself is a finding
Display rounding 1 decimal place for salary and percentages readability
Currency nominal tomans, without inflation adjustment no adjusted data available
Reproducibility random seed 42 and fixed order re-running gives the same result

2.7. Ethical considerations and license #

The data is public and anonymous, but it carries three ethical considerations: (1) analysis of gender and age can help reproduce discrimination; we carry it out to identify gaps, not to prescribe discrimination. (2) Postings are employer preferences and are not attributed to actual workers. (3) The CC BY-NC 4.0 license does not permit commercial use and the source must be cited.
