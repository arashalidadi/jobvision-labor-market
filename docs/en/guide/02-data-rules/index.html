2. Data, data culture and analysis rules

6 min read · 1019 words

2.1. Source and specifications #

The data has been obtained from JobVision's public Hugging Face repository. Each row is a published job posting; not a company, not a job seeker and not a successful hire. This distinction is critical for interpreting all results.

Table 2.1 | Raw data specifications

Characteristic Value Description
Source JobVision Jobposts Dataset Public release on Hugging Face
Number of rows 364,838 Each row is a posting
Number of columns 43 Text, numeric, boolean and JSON
Time span March 2022 to March 2024 Monthly resolution
Complete months 23 months The first and last months are incomplete
Salary unit million tomans per month Inferred from the range of values; examined in Section 5
Salary disclosure rate about 33% Only this subset is analysed for salary
License CC BY-NC 4.0 Research and non-commercial, with attribution

2.4. What is not in the data #

Attention to the "absences" is as important as attention to the "presences":

  • The full posting text and job description are absent; only the title, skills, benefits and requirements.
  • Company name has been anonymised; the competition analysis is carried out at the level of company attributes, not brand.
  • The exact publication date is absent; only the month.
  • Job seeker information, successful hires, actual contract salaries and retention rates are absent.
  • Inflation adjustment is not in the data; the values are nominal and comparisons over time must be interpreted with an inflation index.
  • Hourly wages are not recorded directly; they must be derived from monthly salary and working hours.

2.5. The salary unit: why million tomans? #

The data card states the salary unit as "Toman", but the range of values (2 to 100 with a median of 10) is not compatible with raw "tomans": a monthly salary of 10 tomans is meaningless. Three pieces of evidence show that the real scale is million tomans per month:

  1. The median minimum declared salary is 10 and the median maximum is 12; this is consistent with the market wage level in 1401–1402.
  2. The 99th percentile of the minimum salary is 35 and the maximum is 70; a range that is reasonable for managers and specialist jobs in that same year.
  3. If the scale were "thousand tomans", a median of 10 thousand tomans per month would not be consistent with any standard.

This assumption is applied uniformly throughout the notebook. If the scale of the data changes, all salary figures must be recomputed.

2.6. Pre-defined analysis rules #

To prevent discretionary decisions taken after seeing the result, the following rules are set before the analysis:

Table 2.4 | Fixed analysis rules

Topic Rule Reason
Main period 2022-04 to 2024-02 Removes the two incomplete months at the start and end
Salary unit million tomans per month Consistency with external evidence
Significance level 0.05 Hypothesis tests
Minimum table sample 30 Stability of percentages and medians
High-frequency category threshold 100 postings Sector comparison
Outliers Keep in the description, winsorise 1–99% only in the model Prevent loss of information
Salary missingness Separate analysis, without dropping rows The missingness itself is a finding
Display rounding 1 decimal place for salaries and percentages Readability
Currency Nominal tomans, without inflation adjustment No adjusted data available
Reproducibility Random seed 42 and fixed order Re-running gives the same result

2.7. Ethical considerations and license #

The data is public and anonymous, but it carries three ethical considerations: (1) analysing gender and age can contribute to reproducing discrimination; we carry it out to identify gaps and not to prescribe discrimination. (2) Postings are the employer's preference and are not attributed to the actual workforce. (3) The CC BY-NC 4.0 license does not permit commercial use and the source must be cited.
