Interpretation of Tables 12.1 and 12.2: In postings that disclose a salary, 61.6% do not state a degree level. Among the rest, bachelor's dominates with 30.9%, and master's and PhD together only 3.1%. The median salary rises with a higher degree: 11 for associate degree and undisclosed, 13.5 for bachelor's, 17.5 for master's and PhD; that is, master's and above are about +30% higher than bachelor's.

The important point is in Table 12.2: in postings requiring little experience (zero or one year), the median is 9 for bachelor's and 13.5 million for master's; that is, +50%. The return to a degree on entry to the market is greater than the overall median, because part of the master's postings are for more specialised roles that also accept little experience.

12.2. Regression model for a direct comparison of degree and experience #

import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

model_data = paid.dropna(subset=['salary_mid', 'max_degree', 'Jobpost_RequiredExperienceYears']).copy()
model_data['experience'] = model_data.Jobpost_RequiredExperienceYears.clip(upper=20)
model_data['log_salary'] = np.log(model_data.salary_mid)
model_data['category_model'] = model_data.category_group
model_data['degree_model'] = pd.Categorical(model_data.max_degree,
                                            categories=['کارشناسی', 'اعلام نشده', 'کاردانی', 'کارشناسی ارشد', 'دکترا'])
earnings_model = smf.ols(
    'log_salary ~ experience + I(experience ** 2) + C(degree_model) + C(category_model) + C(province_group) '
    '+ company_size_rank', data=model_data).fit()

degree_terms = [term for term in earnings_model.params.index if term.startswith('C(degree_model)')]
experience_terms = [term for term in earnings_model.params.index if term.startswith(('experience', 'I(experience'))]
selected = degree_terms + experience_terms
coefficient_table = pd.DataFrame({
    'ضریب': earnings_model.params[selected],
    'خطای معیار': earnings_model.bse[selected],
    'p-value': earnings_model.pvalues[selected],
})
coefficient_table['اثر تقریبی بر حقوق'] = (np.exp(coefficient_table['ضریب']) - 1) * 100
coefficient_table.index = [index.replace('C(degree_model)[T.', '').replace(']', '')
                           .replace('experience', 'هر سال سابقه').replace('I(experience ** 2)', 'سابقه به توان دو')
                           for index in coefficient_table.index]
show_table('12.3', 'اثر مدرک و سابقه بر لگاریتم حقوق (مدل رگرسیون خطی)', coefficient_table,
           formats={'ضریب': 'score', 'خطای معیار': 'score', 'p-value': 'pvalue',
                    'اثر تقریبی بر حقوق': 'pp'},
           sample=f'{len(model_data):,} آگهی با حقوق اعلام‌شده',
           note='گروه پایه: مدرک کارشناسی، تهران/سایر، دسته‌های شغلی و اندازه شرکت کنترل‌شده است.')

degree_step = np.exp(earnings_model.params[[term for term in degree_terms
                                            if 'کارشناسی ارشد' in term][0]]) - 1
year_step = np.exp(earnings_model.params['experience']) - 1
comparison_step = pd.DataFrame({'مقدار': [f'{degree_step * 100:,.1f}٪', f'{year_step * 100:,.1f}٪',
                                          f'{degree_step / year_step:,.1f}']},
                               index=['یک پله مدرک (کارشناسی → ارشد)', 'یک سال سابقه بیشتر',
                                      'معادل‌سازی: هر پله مدرک چند سال سابقه'])
show_table('12.4', 'مقایسه ارزش یک پله مدرک با یک سال سابقه', comparison_step,
           sample=f'{len(model_data):,} آگهی', period=False, index_name='مقایسه')

Interpretation of Tables 12.3 and 12.4: In the regression model controlling for job category, province and company size, each year of experience adds +11.1% and each step of degree (bachelor's → master's) +14.9% to salary. The squared experience term is approximately zero (effect −0.1%); therefore, in these data the returns-to-experience curve is almost linear over the range of 0 to 20 years, and the sharp "diminishing returns" seen in Section 8 is mostly the result of a change in the mix of roles at high experience levels, not of experience itself.

Practical conclusion: one step of degree is worth roughly 1.3 years of experience. If an employer is choosing between "a bachelor's with 3 years of experience" and "a master's with 1.7 years of experience", they are roughly equivalent in terms of market pay.

Key figures: education

  • Return on one degree step: 14.9%
  • Return on one year of experience: 11.1%
  • Experience years equivalent to one degree step: 1.3 years
  • R² of the education and experience model: 0.270

Interpretation of Table 12.5 and Figures 12.1 and 12.2: The returns to a degree and to work experience are not identical and depend on the industry:

  • In information technology, the returns to both the degree and experience are around +30% (ratio 1.0); that is, the degree and experience are equivalent.
  • In manufacturing and trade, the return to experience (+50%) is higher than the return to the degree (+29.6%) (ratio 1.7).
  • In internet and e-commerce, the return to experience reaches +94% and the degree +29.6%; that is, experience effectively takes the place of the degree (ratio 3.2).
  • In engineering services and automotive, the return to the degree is zero and the return to experience is positive; in these industries a degree is almost worthless.

The overall pattern: in knowledge-based and mature industries, the degree and experience are equivalent; in fast-growing industries (internet) and traditional industries (manufacturing, automotive, engineering), experience is more valuable.
