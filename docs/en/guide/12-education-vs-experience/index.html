Interpretation of Tables 12.1 and 12.2: in postings that disclose a salary, 61.6% do not state a degree level. Among the rest, bachelor's dominates with 30.9%, and master's and PhD together only 3.1%. The median salary rises with a higher degree: 11 for associate degree and undisclosed, 13.5 for bachelor's, 17.5 for master's and PhD; that is, master's and above are about +30% higher than bachelor's.

The important point is in Table 12.2: in postings requiring little experience (zero or one year), the median is 9 for bachelor's and 13.5 million for master's; that is, +50%. The return to a degree on entry to the market is greater than the overall median, because part of the master's postings are for more specialised roles that also accept little experience.

12.2. Regression model for a direct comparison of degree and experience #

import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

model_data = paid.dropna(subset=['salary_mid', 'max_degree', 'Jobpost_RequiredExperienceYears']).copy()
model_data['experience'] = model_data.Jobpost_RequiredExperienceYears.clip(upper=20)
model_data['log_salary'] = np.log(model_data.salary_mid)
model_data['category_model'] = model_data.category_group
model_data['degree_model'] = pd.Categorical(model_data.max_degree,
                                            categories=['کارشناسی', 'اعلام نشده', 'کاردانی', 'کارشناسی ارشد', 'دکترا'])
earnings_model = smf.ols(
    'log_salary ~ experience + I(experience ** 2) + C(degree_model) + C(category_model) + C(province_group) '
    '+ company_size_rank', data=model_data).fit()

degree_terms = [term for term in earnings_model.params.index if term.startswith('C(degree_model)')]
experience_terms = [term for term in earnings_model.params.index if term.startswith(('experience', 'I(experience'))]
selected = degree_terms + experience_terms
coefficient_table = pd.DataFrame({
    'ضریب': earnings_model.params[selected],
    'خطای معیار': earnings_model.bse[selected],
    'p-value': earnings_model.pvalues[selected],
})
coefficient_table['اثر تقریبی بر حقوق'] = (np.exp(coefficient_table['ضریب']) - 1) * 100
coefficient_table.index = [index.replace('C(degree_model)[T.', '').replace(']', '')
                           .replace('experience', 'هر سال سابقه').replace('I(experience ** 2)', 'سابقه به توان دو')
                           for index in coefficient_table.index]
show_table('12.3', 'اثر مدرک و سابقه بر لگاریتم حقوق (مدل رگرسیون خطی)', coefficient_table,
           formats={'ضریب': 'score', 'خطای معیار': 'score', 'p-value': 'pvalue',
                    'اثر تقریبی بر حقوق': 'pp'},
           sample=f'{len(model_data):,} آگهی با حقوق اعلام‌شده',
           note='گروه پایه: مدرک کارشناسی، تهران/سایر، دسته‌های شغلی و اندازه شرکت کنترل‌شده است.')

degree_step = np.exp(earnings_model.params[[term for term in degree_terms
                                            if 'کارشناسی ارشد' in term][0]]) - 1
year_step = np.exp(earnings_model.params['experience']) - 1
comparison_step = pd.DataFrame({'مقدار': [f'{degree_step * 100:,.1f}٪', f'{year_step * 100:,.1f}٪',
                                          f'{degree_step / year_step:,.1f}']},
                               index=['یک پله مدرک (کارشناسی → ارشد)', 'یک سال سابقه بیشتر',
                                      'معادل‌سازی: هر پله مدرک چند سال سابقه'])
show_table('12.4', 'مقایسه ارزش یک پله مدرک با یک سال سابقه', comparison_step,
           sample=f'{len(model_data):,} آگهی', period=False, index_name='مقایسه')

Interpretation of Tables 12.3 and 12.4: in the regression model controlling for job category, province and company size, each year of experience adds +11.1% and each step of degree (bachelor's → master's) +14.9% to salary. The squared experience term is approximately zero (effect −0.1%); therefore, in these data the returns-to-experience curve is almost linear over the range of 0 to 20 years, and the sharp “diminishing returns” seen in Section 8 is mostly the result of a change in the mix of roles at high experience levels, not of experience itself.

Practical conclusion: one step of degree is worth roughly 1.3 years of experience. If an employer is choosing between “a bachelor's with 3 years of experience” and “a master's with 1.7 years of experience”, they are roughly equivalent in terms of market pay.

Interpretation of table 12.5 and figures 12.1 and 12.2: The returns to degree and to work experience are not the same and depend on the industry:

  • In information technology, the returns to both degree and work experience are about +30% (ratio 1.0); that is, degree and experience are equivalent.
  • In manufacturing and trade, the return to work experience (+50%) is greater than the return to degree (+29.6%) (ratio 1.7).
  • In internet and e-commerce, the return to work experience reaches +94% and degree +29.6%; that is, experience practically takes the place of a degree (ratio 3.2).
  • In engineering services and automotive, the return to degree is zero and the return to work experience is positive; in these industries a degree is almost worthless.

Overall pattern: in knowledge-based and mature industries, degree and work experience are equivalent; in fast-growing industries (internet) and traditional industries (manufacturing, automotive, engineering), experience is worth more.

12.5. Returns to Work Experience and Degree Across Salary Deciles #

Tables 12.3 and 12.4 gave the “mean” returns. But the market is not one market: the return to work experience in the bottom decile (lower-paid roles) differs from the top decile (specialist roles). Quantile regression shows exactly this: for each quantile, a separate coefficient is estimated. If the return to work experience is lower in the upper deciles, it means that at the top of the market, experience is rewarded less.

# ۱۲.۵ — رگرسیون چندکی: بازده سابقه و مدرک در چندک‌های مختلف حقوق
qreg_frame = paid.loc[paid.Jobpost_RequiredExperienceYears.notna()].copy()
qreg_frame['exp_years'] = qreg_frame.Jobpost_RequiredExperienceYears
qreg_frame['size_rank'] = qreg_frame.company_size_rank.fillna(qreg_frame.company_size_rank.median())
qreg_frame['hours'] = qreg_frame.weekly_hours.fillna(qreg_frame.weekly_hours.median())
qreg_frame['degree_rank'] = qreg_frame.max_degree_rank.fillna(0)
qreg_frame = qreg_frame.sample(min(60000, len(qreg_frame)), random_state=RANDOM_STATE)

qreg_rows = []
for quantile in [.1, .25, .5, .75, .9]:
    fit = smf.quantreg('log_salary ~ exp_years + size_rank + hours + degree_rank', qreg_frame).fit(q=quantile)
    qreg_rows.append({'چندک': fa_num(int(quantile * 100)) + '٪',
                      'حدود میانهٔ حقوق (میلیون)': math.exp(fit.params['Intercept']),
                      'بازده هر سال سابقه (٪)': (math.exp(fit.params['exp_years']) - 1) * 100,
                      'بازده هر پله مدرک (٪)': (math.exp(fit.params['degree_rank']) - 1) * 100,
                      'اثر هر پله اندازه شرکت (٪)': (math.exp(fit.params['size_rank']) - 1) * 100,
                      'خطای معیار سابقه': fit.bse['exp_years']})
qreg_table = pd.DataFrame(qreg_rows).set_index('چندک')
show_table('12.6', 'بازده سابقه و مدرک در چندک‌های مختلف حقوق (رگرسیون چندکی)', qreg_table,

Key numbers: returns by decile

  • Return to experience in the bottom decile (10%): 11.1%
  • Return to experience at the median: 12.6%
  • Return to experience in the top decile (90%): 11.9%
  • Return to degree in the bottom decile: 0.7%
  • Return to degree in the top decile: -2.2%

Interpretation of table 12.6 and figure 12.3: The return to work experience is almost uniform across the distribution: 11.1% in the bottom decile, 12.6% at the median and 11.9% in the top decile (the 90% percentile). That is, in the Iranian market “each year of work experience” has almost the same price at all salary levels, and the steeply declining return seen in figure 8.3 is more the result of pooling the categories than a within-decile rule.

By contrast, the return to degree inverts with salary level: +0.7% in the bottom decile (positive but small) and −2.2% in the top decile (negative). Interpretation: at the top of the market, roles are filled by expertise and work experience, not by a degree; someone who earns a higher salary in the top decile with a higher degree is in fact in a more specialised role, and the degree effect is hidden beneath that effect.

An important point for job seekers: investing in a degree has a small return at the bottom of the market and is almost ineffective at the top; investing in work experience has a similar return at all levels. This is consistent with the finding of section 12.4 (a degree is valuable in postings that require little experience): a degree is an “entry door”, not an engine of growth.
