The question of this section is simple: what shape do the key variables have? The answer determines whether we use the median or the mean, and which values are outliers.

Interpretation of table 7.1 and figures 7.1 to 7.4: The salary distribution is right-skewed: median 11 and mean 13.4 million tomans, with a skewness of 2.68. The 2.4 million gap between the mean and the median is itself the best evidence that the “mean” is misleading in this data; a manager who takes the market mean as the salary basis would offer above the market for half of the roles. For this reason, the median and quartiles are reported in all sections.

Taking the logarithm makes the distribution approximately normal (skewness falls from 2.68 to 0.43), and this is the technical justification for using the logarithm of salary as the target variable in section 16.

The distribution of hourly income is also skewed but its range is narrower: median 63.5 and the 99th percentile about 300 thousand tomans per hour. Requested work experience is also skewed: median 2 years, third quartile 3 years, and 15.8% of job postings need no experience; in contrast, only 732 job postings ask for more than 10 years of experience. The overall picture is that Iran's labor market is a “low-experience” market: it usually wants fresh workers or those with two to three years.

Figure 7.4 shows that the experience distribution is concentrated at 0 to 3 years and thins out quickly after 5 years; this same pattern is examined in section 8 as a clue to the diminishing returns of experience.

7.2. Identifying outliers #

Python · 21 lines
def iqr_bounds(series, factor=1.5):
    q1, q3 = series.quantile(.25), series.quantile(.75)
    iqr = q3 - q1
    return q1 - factor * iqr, q3 + factor * iqr


outlier_rows = []
for series, label in [(paid.salary_mid, 'میانه بازه حقوق'), (hourly_ready.hourly_income, 'درآمد ساعتی'),
                      (ads.Jobpost_RequiredExperienceYears, 'سابقه لازم')]:
    low, high = iqr_bounds(series)
    flagged = (series < low) | (series > high)
    outlier_rows.append({'متغیر': label, 'تعداد معتبر': len(series),
                         'کران پایین': low, 'کران بالا': high,
                         'تعداد پرت': int(flagged.sum()), 'سهم پرت': flagged.mean() * 100,
                         'سهم بالای کران': (series > high).mean() * 100})
outlier_table = pd.DataFrame(outlier_rows).set_index('متغیر')
show_table('7.2', 'پرت‌ها بر پایه قاعده ۱٫۵ برابر دامنه میان‌چارکی', outlier_table,
           formats={'تعداد معتبر': 'int', 'کران پایین': 'money2', 'کران بالا': 'money2',
                    'تعداد پرت': 'int', 'سهم پرت': 'pct', 'سهم بالای کران': 'pct'},
           sample='داده در دسترس هر متغیر',
           note='پرت‌ها حذف نمی‌شوند؛ فقط در مدل‌سازی سطح حقوق وینزور می‌شوند.')

Interpretation of table 7.2: The 1.5-times-interquartile-range rule flags 2,382 job postings (2%) above the 30.3 million-toman bound and 115 job postings at the floor (2.5 million or less). This share is small, and removing them would not change the composition of the market, but we do not recommend removing them: a 60-million CEO job posting or a 2.5-million intern posting are both market realities. In description, we keep them all, and in modeling we winsorize only the 1st and 99th percentiles so that a single row cannot shift a regression coefficient.

Interpretation of tables 7.3 and 7.4: The requested experience reaches its ceiling very quickly: 52% of job postings ask for at most 2 years of experience and only about 4% for more than 5 years. On the salary side, the median goes from about 11 million for job postings with no experience requirement to 13.5 million for job postings with 3 years of experience; that is, the biggest jump in pay occurs in the first two years, after which the slope flattens. This is the same “diminishing returns of experience” pattern that is charted in section 8.

The requested age range is stated in only 79.5% of job postings and even there it is narrow: median 30 years and quartiles 28.5 to 34 years. That is, Iranian employers mainly want young to middle-aged workers, and requests for over 40 years are very rare. This pattern returns in section 13 as a market limitation and in section 22 as an operational warning (avoid artificially restricting the age range).

7.4. How much can we trust the medians? Confidence intervals via bootstrap resampling #

All the numbers in this project come from a sample of job postings, so every median is an estimate, not a definite fact. For this reason, we estimate the 95% confidence interval of the medians using bootstrap resampling (bootstrap, 400 repetitions): job postings are resampled with replacement, and the spread of the median across these samples shows the uncertainty. A narrow number means “this number can be attributed to the market”.

# ۷.۴ — فاصلهٔ اطمینان میانه و میانگین با بازنمونه‌گیری
BOOTSTRAP_ROUNDS = 400
boot_rng = np.random.default_rng(RANDOM_STATE)


def bootstrap_ci(values, kind, rounds=BOOTSTRAP_ROUNDS, chunk=40):
    """فاصلهٔ اطمینان ۹۵٪ برای میانه، میانگین یا سهم بالای ۱۵ میلیون."""
    array = np.asarray(pd.Series(values).dropna(), dtype=float)

    def statistic(block, axis=1):
        if kind == 'median':
            return np.median(block, axis=axis)
        if kind == 'mean':
            return block.mean(axis=axis)
        return (block > 15).mean(axis=axis) * 100

    draws_list = []
    for start in range(0, rounds, chunk):
        size = min(chunk, rounds - start)
        draws = boot_rng.integers(0, len(array), size=(size, len(array)))
        draws_list.append(statistic(array[draws]))
        del draws
    distribution = np.concatenate(draws_list)
    point = float(statistic(array.reshape(1, -1))[0])
    return point, float(np.percentile(distribution, 2.5)), float(np.percentile(distribution, 97.5))

Key numbers: estimate confidence

  • Market-wide median salary: 11.0 million (interval width: 0.00)
  • Mean salary (bounds): 13.34 to 13.43 million
  • Median hourly income ± half-width: 63.5 ± 0.00
  • Salary disclosure rate ± half-width: 33.1% ± 0.15 units

Interpretation of table 7.5: The result of the bootstrap resampling is itself a finding, not just a technical check.

  • The monthly median salary has a confidence interval width of zero: in all 400 resamplings, the median stayed exactly 11.0 million. The reason is what we saw in section 9.4: the market has anchored numbers, and the median sits on the anchors. That means this number can be confidently attributed to the whole market.
  • The mean is the only index with a non-zero confidence interval: 13.34 to 13.43 million (width 0.08). Because the right tail extends to 85 million, the mean is sensitive to a few expensive postings; this is the technical reason why the reports throughout this project are based on the median.
  • The disclosure rate and the share of postings above 15 million (26.7%, bounds 26.5 to 27.0) have an interval of less than half a unit — meaning these ratios can be attributed to the whole market.
  • At the category level, the median hourly income of professional sales has the widest interval (70.8 to 74.2, width 3.4) because the roles within this category are very diverse; in contrast, finance and accounting is more compact with a width of 1.4.

Practical implication: The main uncertainty in this project does not come from “data dispersion” but from the sample size of the groups (section 9.4). So wherever a group's sample is small, caution is needed; the medians themselves do not need a margin of error.
