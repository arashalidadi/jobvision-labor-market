Interpretation of table 23.2: The answers to the four management questions, without technical jargon and with reference to the evidence, at a glance:

  • Compensation package: the first-to-third quartile range of each category (22.1) is the competitive basis; the overall market median is 11 million tomans and specialized categories reach 17.5 million.
  • Season and skill: peak publication in Dey (123%) and lowest in Bahman (79%); the highest skill premium within its own category, AutoCAD (+14.4%).
  • Employer competition: only 33.1% of postings are transparent and Tehran holds 64.6% of the market; three levers of differentiation: salary disclosure, explicit benefits and publication timing.
  • Value creation capacity: five applications — pricing, hiring calendar, skill map, gap monitoring and employer benchmarking; the model with R2=0.44 and the network with 4 skill communities.

These answers are the starting point of the conversation with the manager, not its end; each answer can be reviewed in its reference section.

Summary of the recommended package: From five layers of analysis — price (22.1), calendar (22.3), skill (22.4), job requirements (section 12) and transparency (section 9) — an executive package was built, which appears in table 22.5 and in the executive summary of section 23. Three decisions with the greatest impact:

  1. Disclosing the salary range with a width of 20 to 25 percent; it both creates differentiation and makes expectations realistic.
  2. Targeting the median to the third quartile of the job category instead of the overall market mean; because of skewness, the mean is more expensive than necessary for half of the roles.
  3. Publishing in a low-competition month for non-urgent roles (Khordad, Shahrivar, Bahman) and adding two rare benefits (flexible hours, equity incentives) to the four standard benefits.
