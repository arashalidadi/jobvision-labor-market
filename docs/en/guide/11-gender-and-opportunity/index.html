Interpretive warning: the data record the employer's preference in the posting text, not the gender of the person hired and not individual salaries. Therefore any difference we see is a difference between posting categories, not a pay gap between individuals. This distinction is observed in every interpretation in this section.

Interpretation of Tables 11.1 and 11.2 and Figure 11.1: in 55.7% of postings there is no gender preference; 33.5% of postings carry an explicit “men only” or “women only” requirement and the rest are soft preferences. The ratio of “men only” to “women only” postings is 1.61; that is, at the posting level the market is more closed toward men.

But the more important pattern is occupational segregation: in the technical technician and repair technician category 79.1% of postings have a gender requirement (77.4% men only) and in general labourer 68% (56.8% men only); by contrast, in office administrator and administrative clerk 63.2% of postings have a requirement, of which 56.5% is “women only”. In programming only 4.3% of postings have a gender requirement. That is, discrimination lies less in “pay for the same role” and more in “access to roles”.

from scipy.stats import ttest_ind, mannwhitneyu

male_only = paid.loc[paid.gender_pref.eq('فقط آقا'), 'salary_mid'].dropna()
female_only = paid.loc[paid.gender_pref.eq('فقط خانم'), 'salary_mid'].dropna()
no_pref = paid.loc[paid.gender_pref.eq('بدون ترجیح'), 'salary_mid'].dropna()

t_stat, t_p = ttest_ind(male_only, female_only, equal_var=False)
u_stat, u_p = mannwhitneyu(male_only, female_only)
pooled_sd = math.sqrt((male_only.var() + female_only.var()) / 2)
cohens_d = (male_only.mean() - female_only.mean()) / pooled_sd
median_gap = (male_only.median() / female_only.median() - 1) * 100

test_table = pd.DataFrame({
    'مقدار': [f'{len(male_only):,}', f'{len(female_only):,}', f'{male_only.median():,.1f}',

Interpretation of Tables 11.4 and 11.5: when we compute the gap within job categories, it remains around +22 to +23% in most categories (finance, sales, administrative, industrial); that is, occupational composition does not explain the whole difference. Two exceptions matter: in programming the gap reaches +59% (median 17.5 versus 11), but there are only 39 “women only” postings in this category and the sample is small; in general labourer the gap is zero (both 9 million).

At the seniority level, the gap widens as the level rises: bachelor's +22.7%, supervisory +94.4% and managerial +104.5%. This pattern shows that the higher we go up the levels, the fewer female-oriented postings there are and the lower the salaries they offer; the same “glass ceiling” at the posting level.

Serious limitation: all these numbers are the employer's preference in the posting text, not the actual pay of individuals. The result must be read as a “posting market pattern”, not as proof of pay discrimination.

Interpretation of Table 11.3 and Figure 11.2: the median salary of “men only” postings is 11 million and of “women only” postings 9 million tomans; a difference of +22.2%. Welch's t-test (t=42.5) and the Mann–Whitney rank test both find this difference significant at the 0.001 level, and Cohen's effect size (d=0.41) falls in the “medium” range.

Correct interpretation: this difference is not a pay gap between individuals. Female-oriented postings are concentrated more in low-paying categories (administrative, sales, services) and male-oriented postings in technical and industrial roles. A large part of this 22% is an occupational-composition effect, not identical pricing for an identical role. To separate the two, one must look at the gap within job categories; Section 11.3 does exactly that.

gap_rows = []
for category in ads.category_group.value_counts().head(10).index:
    subset = paid.loc[paid.category_group.eq(category)]
    male = subset.loc[subset.gender_pref.eq('فقط آقا'), 'salary_mid']
    female = subset.loc[subset.gender_pref.eq('فقط خانم'), 'salary_mid']
    if len(male) < MIN_N or len(female) < MIN_N:
        gap_rows.append({'دسته شغلی': category, 'تعداد فقط آقا': len(male), 'تعداد فقط خانم': len(female),
                         'میانه فقط آقا': np.nan, 'میانه فقط خانم': np.nan, 'شکاف': np.nan})
        continue
    gap_rows.append({'دسته شغلی': category, 'تعداد فقط آقا': len(male), 'تعداد فقط خانم': len(female),
                     'میانه فقط آقا': male.median(), 'میانه فقط خانم': female.median(),
                     'شکاف': (male.median() / female.median() - 1) * 100})
gap_table = pd.DataFrame(gap_rows).set_index('دسته شغلی').sort_values('شکاف', ascending=False)
show_table('11.4', 'شکاف میانه حقوق میان آگهی‌های مرد‌محور و زن‌محور در دسته‌های پرتکرار', gap_table,
           formats={'تعداد فقط آقا': 'int', 'تعداد فقط خانم': 'int', 'میانه فقط آقا': 'money',
                    'میانه فقط خانم': 'money', 'شکاف': 'pp'},
           sample='دسته‌هایی با دست‌کم ۳۰ آگهی در هر گروه', period=False, index_name='دسته شغلی')

seniority_gap = []
for level in SENIORITY_ORDER:
    subset = paid.loc[paid.seniority.eq(level)]
