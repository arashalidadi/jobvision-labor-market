17. Clustering job titles by skills and education

6 min read · 6 tables · 2 figures · 6 code cells

Question: which job titles are close to one another in terms of their “skill and education basket”? To answer, we turn each job title into a vector: the share of that title's postings that request each skill, together with the share of postings requiring a master's degree or higher. We then cluster the titles.

Python · 24 lines
TITLE_MIN_ADS = 50
title_counts = ads.base_title.value_counts()
eligible_titles = title_counts[title_counts.ge(TITLE_MIN_ADS)].index
top_skills_list = skills_long.skill.value_counts().head(40).index

skill_by_title = (skills_long.loc[skills_long.ad_id.isin(ads.ad_id) & skills_long.skill.isin(top_skills_list)]
                  .merge(ads[['ad_id', 'base_title']], on='ad_id')
                  .groupby(['base_title', 'skill']).ad_id.nunique().unstack(fill_value=0)
                  .reindex(columns=top_skills_list, fill_value=0))
title_ads = ads.loc[ads.base_title.isin(eligible_titles)].groupby('base_title').size()
skill_share = skill_by_title.div(title_ads, axis=0).reindex(eligible_titles).fillna(0)

degree_share = (ads.loc[ads.base_title.isin(eligible_titles)]
                .assign(higher_degree=ads.max_degree.isin(['کارشناسی ارشد', 'دکترا']))
                .groupby('base_title').higher_degree.mean().rename('سهم مدرک ارشد و بالاتر'))
experience_median = (ads.loc[ads.base_title.isin(eligible_titles)]
                     .groupby('base_title').Jobpost_RequiredExperienceYears.median().rename('میانه سابقه'))
title_features = skill_share.join(degree_share).join(experience_median)
show_metrics('داده خوشه‌بندی', [
    ('عنوان با حداقل ۵۰ آگهی', f'{len(eligible_titles):,}'),
    ('آگهی پوشش‌داده‌شده', f'{int(title_ads.sum()):,} ({title_ads.sum() / len(ads) * 100:.1f}٪)'),
    ('مهارت در بردار', f'{len(top_skills_list)}'),
    ('ابعاد بردار هر عنوان', f'{title_features.shape[1]}'),
])

Key figures: clustering data

  • Titles with at least 50 postings: 762
  • Covered postings: 254,451 (70.7%)
  • Skills in the vector: 40
  • Vector dimensions per title: 42

Interpreting the clustering data: 762 job titles with at least 50 postings were selected, covering 70.7% of postings overall (254,451 postings). The vector of each title has 42 dimensions: the share of 40 frequent skills, the share of master's degrees and median experience. Before clustering, the skill vector is weighted with TF-IDF so that ubiquitous skills (Excel and Word) do not take over the whole space — without this step, all titles would fall into one giant cluster.

17.1. Choosing the number of clusters #

from sklearn.cluster import KMeans
from sklearn.metrics import silhouette_score, adjusted_rand_score
from sklearn.decomposition import PCA
from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import TfidfTransformer

skill_weights = TfidfTransformer().fit_transform(skill_share.values).toarray()
degree_block = StandardScaler().fit_transform(
    title_features[['سهم مدرک ارشد و بالاتر', 'میانه سابقه']].values)
X_title = np.hstack([skill_weights, degree_block])
k_scores = []
for k in range(3, 9):
    kmeans = KMeans(n_clusters=k, n_init=20, random_state=RANDOM_STATE).fit(X_title)
    k_scores.append({'k': k, 'اینرسی': kmeans.inertia_,
                     'سیلوئت': silhouette_score(X_title, kmeans.labels_, sample_size=min(2000, len(X_title)),
                                                 random_state=RANDOM_STATE)})
k_table = pd.DataFrame(k_scores).set_index('k')
show_table('17.1', 'انتخاب تعداد خوشه با معیار سیلوئت', k_table,
           formats={'اینرسی': 'hourly', 'سیلوئت': 'ratio'},
           sample=f'{len(title_features)} عنوان شغلی', period=False, index_name='k',
           note='سیلوئت نزدیک به ۱ خوشه‌بندی تمیز و نزدیک به ۰ مرزهای مبهم را نشان می‌دهد.')

best_k = int(k_table['سیلوئت'].idxmax())
title_model = KMeans(n_clusters=best_k, n_init=50, random_state=RANDOM_STATE).fit(X_title)
title_features_clustered = title_features.copy()
title_features_clustered['خوشه'] = title_model.labels_

fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(15, 5.2), gridspec_kw={'width_ratios': [1, 1.15]})
ax = axes[0]
ax.plot(k_table.index, k_table['سیلوئت'], marker='o', color=TEAL)
ax.axvline(best_k, color=GOLD, linestyle='--', linewidth=1.4)
ax.set_xlabel(fa('تعداد خوشه'))
ax.set_ylabel(fa('امتیاز سیلوئت'))
heading(ax, '۱۷.۱', 'انتخاب تعداد خوشه')
ax = axes[1]
coords = PCA(n_components=2, random_state=RANDOM_STATE).fit_transform(X_title)
for cluster in range(best_k):
    mask = title_model.labels_ == cluster
    ax.scatter(coords[mask, 0], coords[mask, 1], s=28, alpha=0.75,
               color=PALETTE[cluster % len(PALETTE)], label=fa(f'خوشه {cluster + 1}'))
biggest_titles = title_ads.reindex(title_features.index).sort_values(ascending=False).head(12).index
for name in biggest_titles:
    position = list(title_features.index).index(name)
    ax.annotate(fa(name), (coords[position, 0], coords[position, 1]), fontsize=7,
                textcoords='offset points', xytext=(4, 3))
ax.set_xlabel(fa('مؤلفه اصلی اول'))
ax.set_ylabel(fa('مؤلفه اصلی دوم'))
ax.legend(fontsize=8)
heading(ax, '۱۷.۲', 'نقشه دوبعدی عناوین شغلی')

Interpreting Table 17.1 and Figures 17.1 and 17.2: The silhouette criterion selects k=3 with a score of 0.510. The two-dimensional map shows that the space has one large “general mass” and two “specialist islands”; not several clusters of equal size. This is itself a finding: at the skill level, Iran's market of job titles does not have high cluster diversity, and a large part of the roles is run with one shared skill basket (office software + administrative communication).

Interpreting Table 17.2 and Figure 17.3: The three clusters are three different worlds:

  • Cluster 1 (642 titles, 233,638 postings, 91.8%): the general mass of the market — sales, accounting, administration, services. The dominant skill basket: Excel, Word, PowerPoint; median salary 11 million, experience 2 years and a share of master's degrees of only 2%.
  • Cluster 2 (107 titles, 19,413 postings): senior finance and accounting roles — senior accountant, finance manager, head of accounting. Median salary 17.5 million, experience 5 years and 15.4% requiring a master's or higher.
  • Cluster 3 (13 titles, 1,400 postings): technical/regulatory roles — technical officer, regulatory affairs specialist, insurance agency. Median salary 17.5 million and 53.5% requiring a master's or higher.

Managerial message: the main difference between the clusters is not in “software skill”, it is in seniority level and degree. To promote a role from cluster 1 to cluster 2, adding one new skill is not enough; the combination of experience and degree is what decides.

17.3. Have the cluster boundaries changed over the two years? #

Show the code for this step56 lines
Python
from scipy.optimize import linear_sum_assignment


def cluster_by_year(year, columns):
    frame = ads.loc[ads.jyear.eq(year) & ads.base_title.isin(eligible_titles)]
    counts = frame.groupby('base_title').size()
    keep = counts[counts.ge(30)].index
    skills_year = (skills_long.loc[skills_long.ad_id.isin(frame.ad_id) & skills_long.skill.isin(top_skills_list)]
                   .merge(ads[['ad_id', 'base_title']], on='ad_id')
                   .groupby(['base_title', 'skill']).ad_id.nunique().unstack(fill_value=0)
                   .reindex(columns=top_skills_list, fill_value=0))
    share = skills_year.div(counts, axis=0).fillna(0).reindex(keep)
    degree = (frame.assign(higher=frame.max_degree.isin(['کارشناسی ارشد', 'دکترا']))
              .groupby('base_title')[['higher']].mean().reindex(keep))
    experience = frame.groupby('base_title').Jobpost_RequiredExperienceYears.median().reindex(keep)
    matrix = share.join(degree.rename(columns={'higher': 'سهم مدرک ارشد و بالاتر'})).join(
        experience.rename('میانه سابقه')).fillna(0)
    return StandardScaler().fit_transform(matrix.values), matrix.index


X_1401, titles_1401 = cluster_by_year(1401, title_features.columns)
X_1402, titles_1402 = cluster_by_year(1402, title_features.columns)
labels_1401 = KMeans(n_clusters=best_k, n_init=30, random_state=RANDOM_STATE).fit_predict(X_1401)
labels_1402 = KMeans(n_clusters=best_k, n_init=30, random_state=RANDOM_STATE).fit_predict(X_1402)

common_titles = titles_1401.intersection(titles_1402)
position_1401 = {title: index for index, title in enumerate(titles_1401)}
position_1402 = {title: index for index, title in enumerate(titles_1402)}
matched = pd.DataFrame({
    '1401': [labels_1401[position_1401[title]] for title in common_titles],
    '1402': [labels_1402[position_1402[title]] for title in common_titles]}, index=common_titles)
ari = adjusted_rand_score(matched['1401'], matched['1402'])

contingency = pd.crosstab(matched['1401'], matched['1402'])
row_ind, col_ind = linear_sum_assignment(-contingency.values)
mapping = {contingency.index[row]: contingency.columns[col] for row, col in zip(row_ind, col_ind)}
matched['خوشه هم‌ارز ۱۴۰۲'] = matched['1401'].map(mapping).fillna(matched['1401'])
matched['جابه‌جا شده'] = matched['1401'] != matched['خوشه هم‌ارز ۱۴۰۲']
moved = matched.loc[matched['جابه‌جا شده']]

stability = pd.DataFrame({'مقدار': [f'{len(common_titles):,}', f'{ari:.3f}', f'{len(moved):,}',
                                    f'{len(moved) / len(common_titles) * 100:.1f}٪']},
                         index=['عنوان مشترک دو سال', 'شاخص رند تعدیل‌شده', 'عنوان جابه‌جا‌شده',
                                'سهم جابه‌جایی'])
show_table('17.3', 'پایداری خوشه‌ها بین دو سال', stability,
           sample=f'{len(common_titles)} عنوان مشترک', period=False, index_name='شاخص',
           note='شاخص رند تعدیل‌شده نزدیک به ۱ یعنی خوشه‌بندی دو سال تقریباً یکسان است؛ نزدیک به ۰ یعنی تصادفی.')
show_table('17.4', 'توزیع تعداد عنوان‌ها در تطابق خوشه‌های دو سال', contingency,
           formats={column: 'int' for column in contingency.columns},
           sample=f'{len(common_titles)} عنوان مشترک', period=False, index_name='خوشه ۱۴۰۱')
show_metrics('پایداری خوشه', [
    ('تعداد خوشه انتخابی', str(best_k)),
    ('شاخص رند تعدیل‌شده', f'{ari:.3f}'),
    ('عنوان جابه‌جا‌شده', f'{len(moved):,} از {len(common_titles):,}'),
    ('میانه سیلوئت', f'{k_table["سیلوئت"].max():.3f}'),
])

Interpreting Tables 17.3 and 17.4: Clustering each year independently and matching them gives an adjusted Rand index of 0.952; that is, the cluster boundaries stayed almost identical across the two years and no title moved between equivalent clusters. Table 17.4 also shows that the matching matrix is almost diagonal.

Conclusion: Over the two-year window, the “boundaries between jobs” did not change. New skills (WordPress, Google Analytics) moved around within those same clusters, but they did not cause the clusters to merge or split. This is consistent with the finding in Section 19: the skill basket of a title stays fixed and change is gradual.

Signature of each cluster: Table 17.2 gave the full profile of the clusters. The project brief explicitly asks “what differences they have from one another and which titles are in each cluster”. For a compact answer, for each cluster we show the skills that have the largest distance from the overall mean — that is, exactly what sets the cluster apart from the rest, not the things that are common to all clusters.

Interpreting Table 17.5: The three clusters are three distinct markets:

  • Cluster 1 — the general body of the market (642 titles, 233,638 postings, only 2% with a master's degree): the highest-volume part of the market. Core titles “sales specialist, accountant, accounting specialist” and its distinctive skill is graphics and web (+0.6 units), while Excel in it is 2.2 units below the mean. This cluster is where software skill is in little demand and the roles are general.
  • Cluster 2 — senior finance and accounting (107 titles, 19,413 postings, median experience 5 years, 15% master's): its signature is Excel (+13.5 units) and Word (+9.2 units); core titles “senior accountant, finance manager, head of accounting”. That is, the finance promotion path, contrary to intuition, runs through spreadsheet skills, not specialised tools.
  • Cluster 3 — technical-regulatory roles (13 titles, 1,400 postings, 53.5% master's degrees): the smallest cluster but the most degree-driven part of the whole market (technical officer, regulatory affairs specialist, insurance sales agency). Here a master's degree is effectively an entry requirement, not an advantage.

Clustering summary: The main boundary of the market is “general versus specialised”, not “technical versus non-technical”. The specialised cluster has a small volume (0.5% of postings) but imposes the hardest entry conditions; the finance cluster is experience-driven; and the general cluster is the biggest market for mass recruitment. These three want three different hiring strategies.

Cluster stability with bootstrap resampling: Clustering was performed once on the data; the right question is whether the clusters hold if we reshuffle 20% of the titles, or whether something different emerges each time. Each time, the ARI (Adjusted Rand Index) metric compares the bootstrap clustering with the baseline clustering; above 0.7 means a stable structure.

# ۱۷.۶ — پایداری خوشه‌ها با بازنمونه‌گیری
baseline_labels = title_features_clustered['خوشه'].to_numpy()
stability_rng = np.random.default_rng(RANDOM_STATE)
stability_scores = []
title_count = len(baseline_labels)
for _ in range(25):
    chosen = stability_rng.choice(title_count, size=int(title_count * 0.8), replace=False)
    labels = KMeans(n_clusters=best_k, n_init=10, random_state=RANDOM_STATE).fit_predict(X_title[chosen])
    stability_scores.append(adjusted_rand_score(baseline_labels[chosen], labels))

stability_scores = np.array(stability_scores)
stability_ari_table = pd.DataFrame({
    'مقدار': [float(np.mean(stability_scores)), float(np.std(stability_scores)),
              float(np.percentile(stability_scores, 5)), float(np.min(stability_scores)),
              float((stability_scores >= .7).mean() * 100)],
}, index=['میانگین ARI', 'انحراف معیار', 'صدک پنجم', 'کمینه', 'سهم تکرارهای پایدار (٪)'])
show_table('17.6', 'پایداری خوشه‌ها با بازنمونه‌گیری عنوان‌ها (۲۵ تکرار، ۸۰٪ نمونه)', stability_ari_table,
           formats={'مقدار': 'score'}, sample=f'{title_count} عنوان شغلی، {best_k} خوشه',
           period=False, index_name='شاخص')

show_metrics('پایداری بازنمونه', [
    ('میانگین ARI', f'{stability_scores.mean():.3f}'),
    ('انحراف معیار', f'{stability_scores.std():.3f}'),
    ('کمینهٔ ARI', f'{stability_scores.min():.3f}'),
    ('تکرارهای پایدار (ARI ≥ ۰٫۷)', pf((stability_scores >= .7).mean() * 100, 0)),
])

Key numbers: bootstrap stability

  • Mean ARI: 0.896
  • Standard deviation: 0.217
  • Minimum ARI: 0.304
  • Stable repeats (ARI ≥ 0.7): 88%

Interpretation of Table 17.6: The mean ARI over 25 bootstrap resamples (80% of titles each time) is 0.896 — that is, the clustering is stable and the three-part structure interpreted in Sections 17.2 and 17.5 is not random. But the standard deviation of 0.22 and the minimum of 0.3 show that in some iterations the structure breaks down.

The cause is clear: the third cluster has only 13 titles. With the random removal of 20% of titles, two or three titles may drop out of this cluster and the boundaries shift. By contrast, the two large clusters (642 and 107 titles) remained stable in all iterations.

Verdict: The interpretations concerning the “general market core” and “senior finance” are reliable and generalizable; the interpretation of the small technical-regulatory cluster is directional and is not reported as a definitive fact.
