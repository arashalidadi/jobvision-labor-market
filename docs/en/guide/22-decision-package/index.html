Interpretation of table 22.1: This table is the direct answer to the project's first question: for each job category, the competitive range “first to third quartile” and the target level “median”. For example, programming has a range of 13.5 to 22.5 with a median of 17.5 million (+59% higher than the overall median); by contrast, office administrator and general laborer have a range of 7 to 11 with a median of 9 million (−18%).

Decision rule: if you want to be “competitive in the market”, place the offer close to the median; if you want to be “distinct”, target the third quartile and instead be less strict about the job requirements. If the budget is limited, the first quartile is only suitable for low-competition roles or internships, not for specialized roles.

Interpretation of table 22.3 and figure 22.1: The recommended publication calendar was built from the seasonal index of section 10. For non-urgent roles, the months Khordad (85.5%), Shahrivar (88.2%), Ordibehesht (91.2%) and Bahman (78.9%) have the lowest competition; job postings are seen less in these months, so the chance of being seen is higher. For urgent roles, or when a high volume of job seekers matters, Dey (123%), Mehr (114.7%) and Aban (112.1%) are the best times.

Note: “high competition” is not bad for the employer; it means more job seekers are active in the market. The choice depends on your goal: job posting visibility (low-competition month) or access to a larger volume of candidates (high-competition month).

22.3. Skills that raise the salary #

premium_rows = []
top_skill_candidates = skills_long.skill.value_counts().head(20).index
for skill in top_skill_candidates:
    skill_ids = set(skills_long.loc[skills_long.skill.eq(skill), 'ad_id'])
    with_skill = paid.loc[paid.ad_id.isin(skill_ids)]
    if len(with_skill) < 300:
        continue
    main_category = with_skill.category_group.value_counts().idxmax()
    category_paid = paid.loc[paid.category_group.eq(main_category)]
    inside = category_paid.loc[category_paid.ad_id.isin(skill_ids), 'salary_mid']
    outside = category_paid.loc[~category_paid.ad_id.isin(skill_ids), 'salary_mid']
    if len(inside) < MIN_N or len(outside) < MIN_N:
        continue
    premium_rows.append({'مهارت': skill, 'دسته مقایسه': main_category, 'تعداد دارای مهارت': len(inside),
                         'میانه با مهارت': inside.median(), 'میانه بدون مهارت': outside.median(),
                         'صرفه در دسته': (np.exp(np.log(inside).mean() - np.log(outside).mean()) - 1) * 100})
skill_premium = pd.DataFrame(premium_rows).set_index('مهارت').sort_values('صرفه در دسته', ascending=False)
show_table('22.4', 'صرفه حقوقی مهارت‌ها در دسته شغلی خودشان', skill_premium.head(12),
           formats={'تعداد دارای مهارت': 'int', 'میانه با مهارت': 'money', 'میانه بدون مهارت': 'money',
                    'صرفه در دسته': 'pp'},
           sample=f'{len(paid):,} آگهی با حقوق', index_name='مهارت',
           note='مقایسه درونی هر دسته انجام شده تا اثر ترکیب شغلی تا حد ممکن کنترل شود؛ رابطه توصیفی است، نه علی.')

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(10.5, 5))
plot_premium = skill_premium.head(10).iloc[::-1]
bars = ax.barh(range(len(plot_premium)), plot_premium['صرفه در دسته'], color=TEAL, height=0.65)
ax.set_yticks(range(len(plot_premium)))
ax.set_yticklabels([fa(name) for name in plot_premium.index], fontsize=8.5)
ax.axvline(0, color=DARK, linewidth=0.9)
ax.set_xlabel(fa('اختلاف میانه حقوق با و بدون مهارت (٪)'))
for position, value in enumerate(plot_premium['صرفه در دسته']):
    ax.text(value + 0.4, position, f'{value:+.1f}٪', va='center', fontsize=8)

Interpretation of table 22.4 and figure 22.2: The salary premium of skills was calculated by comparing the mean log salary “with skill” and “without skill” in the same job category. The highest premiums: AutoCAD +14.4%, Microsoft Visio +13.2%, Rahkaran +11.8%, Git +11.4% and Hamkaran System +8.2%. WordPress in digital marketing is +6.1%.

This table is useful for writing a job posting and for salary negotiation: if you want a role with AutoCAD or Git, the market pays a considerable premium for that same skill; by contrast, Excel and Word are basic skills and on their own do not create a premium. Note: this relationship is descriptive; the combination of skill with role and industry can explain part of the premium.

22.4. Effective job posting checklist #

Table 22.5 | Executive checklist for writing and scheduling a job posting

Decision Executive recommendation Evidence in the analysis
Salary disclosure State the salary range; even a wide range is better than “negotiable” Section 9; a disclosure rate of 33% and a structural difference by company size and category
Salary amount Target the median to the third quartile of your own category, not the overall market mean Table 22.1
Publication time Avoid the peak competition months (Mehr and Aban) for non-urgent roles; Esfand and Farvardin are a less crowded opportunity Table 22.3 and figure 22.1
Benefits package State supplementary health insurance, bonus and loan explicitly; these are the market's most frequent expectations Table 6.9
Skill List the skills that carry a premium in the title and in the skills list Table 22.4
Job requirements Limit work experience and degree to the necessary minimum; each degree step is worth several years of experience Section 12
Remote work If the role can be done remotely, highlight it in the posting; the supply of this benefit is low Sections 14 and 15
Geography In provinces other than Tehran the salary range is more competitive and competition is lower Section 14
Job title Write the title simply and standardly; overly detailed titles make searching hard Section 6.8
