From raw ads to decisions

One raw dataset, one hiring manager, and four decisions due this year

A job ad does three things at once: it announces the employer's demand, shows the expected skill level, and — when pay is disclosed — reveals the offered price of labor. Aggregated over 23 months, these ads describe who hires whom, with what skill, where, and at what price.

Question 1

What is a defensible pay package?

For each role family and province, which salary and hourly range is both competitive and defensible?

Question 2

When and with which skills should we hire?

In which months does demand peak, and which skills carry the largest pay premium?

Question 3

How does an ad stand out?

How does competition between employers work, and what do pay disclosure, benefits, and timing really change?

Question 4

What value can this data create?

Five practical uses: pricing labor, a hiring calendar, a skill map, gap monitoring, and employer benchmarking.

Ground rule: every number is reported with its denominator, sample, and limitation. A "median salary" is computed only on ads that disclose pay, while the "disclosure rate" covers all ads; the two are not interchangeable.

Why 360,083 and not 364,838? The first and last months in the file (March 2022 and March 2024) are partial slices. Removing them leaves 23 complete months covering 98.7% of rows.

Ten key findings

What 360,000 job ads reveal about Iran's labor market

Each finding pairs a number with a clean denominator and a reference to the analysis section in the notebook.

01

A market that grew steadily

Monthly volume rose from 12,782 ads in April 2022 to 24,017 in October 2023 — 88% growth in 19 months — with a trough of 5,531 in March 2023.

360,083 postings · sections 4 & 10
02

Thin, but structured, pay transparency

Only 33.1% of ads disclose pay. Disclosure is 43.4% at firms with 11–50 employees and 16.6% at firms with 1,001–5,000; a chi-square test (Cramér's V = 0.19) shows the missingness is not random.

disclosure model: AUC 0.65
03

Median 11 million; the competitive band is 9–17.5

Pay is right-skewed (mean 13.4). Software development sits at a median of 17.5, roughly 59% above the market, while office clerks and general laborers sit near 9, about 18% below.

119,048 ads with disclosed pay
04

Contract work pays more per hour; part-time does not

The median hourly rate is 63.5 thousand toman. Contract and project work reaches 89 thousand, but the apparent part-time premium disappears once explicit hours are used — an estimation error, not a market fact.

116,244 ads with pay and hours
05

One degree level ≈ 1.3 years of experience

Holding role and industry constant, one more year of experience adds 11.1% and one degree level adds 14.9%. For entry-level roles the bachelor-to-master gap reaches 50%.

log-salary regression · section 12
06

Hiring season: Dey, Mehr and Aban

The seasonal index peaks at 123% in Dey (Dec–Jan) with Mehr and Aban at 115% and 112%; the quietest months are Bahman (79%), Khordad (86%) and Shahrivar (88%). Hiring freezes before Nowruz.

two-year average, Persian calendar
07

Occupational segregation is larger than the pay gap

33.5% of ads state a gender requirement. The median "men only" vs "women only" gap is 22.2%, but it reaches 105% at the managerial level, and 79% of technical technician ads are male-only.

t-test and Mann-Whitney, p < 0.001
08

Tehran holds 64.6% of opportunities — without a price gap

The median salary is 11 million across every high-volume province. Remote work, however, is far more common outside Tehran (15–21%) than in it (3.1%), and Tehran's share fell 2.7 points in two years.

32 provinces · section 14
09

Office software is the core of the skill market

Excel appears in 38.4% of all ads. Co-occurrence analysis finds four clear communities: office and Iranian finance software, software development, design and media, and CAD and engineering.

491 skills · 35-node core network
10

The model explains 44% of pay variation

Held-out R² is 0.44 (tree model 0.58). Experience matters most (+12.5% per standard deviation in 1402), and the gap between job families reaches −60%.

reproducible pipeline · section 16

Read the limitations too. This data covers only postings published on JobVision and does not represent Iran's entire labor market; company names are anonymized; age and gender fields express employer preference, not the hired person's attributes; and the salary unit is documented as an assumption. See Limitations.

Business playbook

Six decisions for a hiring manager, backed by evidence

Each recommendation links to a numeric piece of evidence and a table you can audit.

Decision 1 · Price

Anchor on the family median-to-Q3, not the market mean

A mean of 13.4 is skewed by the tail; for half of all roles it overpays. Table 22.1 gives the Q1–Q3 band per family.

Decision 2 · Transparency

Publish a pay band that is 20–25% wide

Only 33.1% of ads are transparent. A band differentiates the ad and anchors expectations without exposing your walk-away price.

Decision 3 · Calendar

Post non-urgent roles in low-competition months

Bahman (79%), Khordad (86%) and Shahrivar (88%) are the quietest. For critical roles, Dey and Mehr are when talent is looking.

Decision 4 · Skills

Price a skill inside its own job family

AutoCAD (+14.4%) and Visio (+13.2%) premiums exist only within their family. A skill list without context carries no price signal.

Decision 5 · Requirements

Ask for realistic experience; keep the degree signal

Each degree level equals 1.3 years of experience, and the bachelor-to-master gap reaches 50% early in careers. Fewer than 4% of ads demand more than 5 years.

Decision 6 · Access

Treat remote work as a sourcing channel

Remote work is 15–21% outside Tehran versus 3.1% inside it. For location-independent roles it multiplies the talent pool.

Method & quality

From 364,838 raw rows to a decision package

Five stages, fixed analysis rules declared up front, and a decision log. Every table and chart carries a unique number, and the notebook ends with an automated audit.

Understand and quality-check

Shape, dtypes, missingness, duplicates, time coverage and cardinality across 43 columns. Without this, every later number is suspect.

Clean under fixed rules

Drop the two partial months, normalize Arabic characters, harmonize key text fields, and log all eight cleaning decisions with their effect.

Engineer features

Weekly hours from free text (97.6% coverage), 491 skills, degrees and fields, 15 benefit types, employer features, and hourly income.

Analyze and test

Distributions and outliers, Pearson and Spearman, chi-square and t-tests, regressions, salary modeling, and Gower-distance clustering.

Audit and reproduce

Fourteen automated checks (monthly totals, ID uniqueness, hour bounds, share sums) plus scripts that rebuild the report and the deck.

Limitations & licensing

What this data does not tell you

Being explicit about limitations is part of the analysis. Seven constraints condition how the results should be read.

The data covers published ads only

It is not the whole Iranian labor market: firms that never post on JobVision, hires without an ad, and informal work are invisible here.

Company names are anonymized

Employer analysis works at the level of firm attributes (size, industry, province, disclosure rate), never brands. The employer typology is an analytical device, not a verdict on a company.

Only one in three ads discloses pay

All salary analysis uses that subset, and its selection bias is tested separately. A disclosure model reaching AUC 0.65 shows the missingness is structured but not easily predictable.

Age and gender are employer preferences

These fields describe what the ad asks for, not who was hired. The true wage gap cannot be inferred from them.

The first and last months are partial

March 2022 and March 2024 are truncated slices and are excluded from time-series work; 23 complete months remain.

Working hours are parsed from free text

Hours were reconstructed for 97.6% of ads, but part of that relies on assumptions about the contract type. The hourly analysis is paired with a robustness check (table 6.11.1).

The salary unit is a documented assumption

The dataset card says "Toman"; the value range (2–100) and the comparison with market wages indicate the real scale is million toman per month. The notebook tests this assumption explicitly.
